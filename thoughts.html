What equity research actually teaches you about thinking

Building a stock pitch from scratch is one of the better thinking exercises I've found.

▼ read more

When you build a stock pitch, you're forced to have a view — not just a summary of what everyone else thinks. The variant view exercise is humbling. Most of the time, your initial "insight" is just consensus with more footnotes.

The discipline is in asking: what would have to be true for this to be wrong? And then stress-testing your own conviction against that. The best pitches don't win on information edge. They win on interpretive edge — the same data, read differently. That's what I'm trying to build.

Six months into AI transformation work: what I've learned

Working on an enterprise AI platform engagement has changed how I think about what makes AI initiatives succeed or fail.

▼ read more

The biggest lesson: the technology is rarely the bottleneck. Most enterprise AI failures are change management failures dressed up as technical ones. The model works. The integration works. But the workflow didn't change, the incentives didn't change, and the people using it never had a reason to trust it.

The projects that work are the ones where someone senior cares enough to make the adjacent process changes — not just the tool deployment.
